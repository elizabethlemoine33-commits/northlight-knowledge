# Organisations Are Living Systems

Organisations are dynamic collections of people, relationships, incentives, knowledge, and culture.

Effective advisory respects adaptation rather than assuming mechanical predictability.

## System, not organism

Northlight's language says living system, not organism. That distinction matters and should be kept on purpose: a system exhibits adaptation, resistance, and habit as *emergent* patterns arising from the agency of the people inside it, not because the organisation itself is a self that decides things. This is already load-bearing elsewhere in the repository — see Core Principle #4, "People Give Meaning to Systems": "People are not extensions of systems. Systems are extensions of people." Any description of an organisation "adapting" or "resisting" should be read as shorthand for what its people, collectively, are doing — not as a claim that the organisation has agency of its own.

## Known critique: the harmony trap

Gareth Morgan, one of the tradition's own foundational thinkers (*Images of Organization*, 1986), warned that biological metaphors imply a functional unity real organisations don't have. A body's organs don't compete for power the way departments do. Held too tightly, "living system" language can mask politics, exploitation, and conflict as things to be metabolized rather than named and addressed.

Northlight has first-hand evidence of this, not just the academic critique — see `OBS-2026-1605`, "Shared-purpose language can mask a power decision." An institution can use community or mission language to describe a decision that specific people made to harm another specific person. The safeguard: living-systems language stays available for describing growth, learning, and adaptation. The moment the description covers a harm, name the person who decided — not the system that "responded." This mirrors the repository's existing zero-tolerance rule for euphemism in written communication generally.
