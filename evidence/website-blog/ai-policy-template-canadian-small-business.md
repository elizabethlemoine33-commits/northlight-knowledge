# AI Acceptable Use Policy Template for Canadian Small Business — Northlight

## Summary

A practical AI acceptable use policy template built for Canadian law (PIPEDA, Quebec Law 25, Ontario ESA) rather than US or EU frameworks. Provides a fill-in-the-blank template covering approved tools, data rules, human review requirements, disclosure obligations, and policy ownership. Explicitly notes this is a starting point, not legal advice.

## Key Claims

- Bill C-27 (AIDA — Artificial Intelligence and Data Act) died in early 2025 when Parliament dissolved. "AI for All" (June 2026 federal strategy) commits existing regulators to use current powers, not create new obligations. There is no comprehensive federal AI law in Canada.
- What does govern you: PIPEDA federally, plus provincial statutes (Quebec Law 25, Alberta PIPA, BC PIPA). Every law that applied to how you treat people and handle their data still applies when AI touches that data. The tool is new; the obligation is not.
- Quebec Law 25 includes transparency requirements for automated decision-making — affects how you can use AI in ways that impact individuals.
- Ontario January 1, 2026: employers with 25+ employees must disclose AI use in publicly advertised job postings when AI is used to screen, assess, or select candidates. This is a live obligation.
- A copied policy does not protect you; a policy that reflects decisions your business actually made does.
- Data test: if you would not email this information to a stranger, confirm it is appropriate for your AI tool before entering it.
- An AI policy without an owner is a document that lives in a folder and gets forgotten. The policy owner maintains the approved tools list, evaluates new tool requests, and updates policy when obligations change.
- Recommended review cadence: quarterly — appropriate given how fast AI tools and regulations are changing.
