# How to Prepare Your Business for AI Adoption: A Canadian Checklist — Northlight

## Summary

A 7-step AI readiness checklist for Canadian SMBs without a data team or VP of Innovation. Opens with the gap between adoption rates and ROI (45% adoption, 2% seeing return) and frames readiness as the missing variable. Covers problem definition, data audit, ownership assignment, legal obligations, pilot structure, guardrails, and change management.

## Key Claims

- 45% of Canadian businesses now use generative AI in some form. Only 2% have seen a return on it (KPMG Canada). The gap is not about tools — it is about readiness.
- The first question is not "What AI should we use?" — it is "What is breaking, slow, or expensive enough that we would pay to change it?"
- Most businesses adopt AI because they feel they should, not because they have identified what it is for. That is fine as a motivation to start researching. It is a terrible place to start spending.
- AI adoption fails when nobody owns it or everyone thinks they do. One accountable person — not a committee — must make decisions, allocate budget, and say no when something is a bad idea.
- Shadow AI often predates formal AI policy: employees using personal AI subscriptions, voice tools, or ChatGPT without organizational knowledge. Data policies may already be violated before they are written. The first question is: what are people already doing?
- 90-day pilot default: shorter gives insufficient data; longer than six months and you are not piloting anymore, you are just using the tool without deciding if it works.
- Guardrails define what AI can do, what requires human review, what data it can access, and who is responsible when it makes a mistake — set these before launch, not after.
- The businesses that succeed with AI treat it as a change management project, not a tech project. The tool is the easy part.
- Ontario January 2026 obligation: employers with 25+ employees must disclose AI use in job postings when AI is used in candidate screening or assessment.
