# AI Adoption — Context Problem and Translation Gap — LinkedIn Posts

## Summary

The sharpest LinkedIn articulation of why AI underperforms in most organizations and what actually fixes it. The posts develop two parallel arguments: that AI underperformance is almost always a context problem (not a tool problem), and that non-adoption is almost always a translation problem (not a cost or fear problem). The posts also surface the pilot-to-rollout gap as a human design problem, not a technical one.

## Source

- Channel: Elizabeth Anne (Randell) Lemoine, LinkedIn personal profile
- Retrieved via: Buffer API
- Date range: May 2026 – July 2026
- Post count: ~10 substantive posts in this cluster

## Key Claims

- "You wouldn't hire a new employee and send them straight to client work without onboarding — but we do exactly that with AI, then wonder why it underperforms." The fix is the unsexy work of mapping where institutional knowledge actually lives and building the connective tissue that lets AI use it.
- Quality of thinking going IN matters more than the tool; the bottleneck for most people isn't access, it's clarity about the problem; if you can't explain what you're trying to solve to another person in plain language, you won't get a useful answer from AI either
- "Save whose time, on what tasks, to do what instead?" — if you don't answer that before implementation, you end up with AI tools that technically work but don't change how anything gets done; someone still needs to own the outcome
- The pilot-to-rollout gap is human not technical: no shared language for what good AI use looks like, no examples from people in similar roles, no one whose job it is to answer questions when something feels off; early adopters succeeded because they had curiosity, autonomy, and time to figure it out — most people aren't in that position
- 78% of Canadian businesses that haven't adopted AI say it's not cost or fear — it's that they can't see how AI applies to what they actually do; that's a translation problem, and no national strategy fixes it; the strategy creates conditions, you still do the thinking
- AI readiness: three questions before the technology conversation — do people understand their own workflows well enough to know where AI would help? Is there someone with authority and genuine interest to own it past the pilot? Does the organisation have a track record of adopting new ways of working, or does everything new get absorbed back into how things have always been done?
- "Most Canadian businesses that have 'tried AI' have actually hired a strategy consultant, received a deck full of recommendations, and been left to implement those recommendations themselves" — that's a category confusion; strategy consultants and implementation partners are not the same engagement; the test: can you show me a project write-up with the problem, the solution, and measurable results?
- Any firm that starts with tool conversations before problem conversations is working backwards; blank stares on PIPEDA and Quebec's Law 25 signal a US firm that hasn't adapted to Canadian conditions
