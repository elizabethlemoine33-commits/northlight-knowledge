# Shared-purpose language can mask a power decision

## Observation

When an institution frames a decision that harms a specific person as something the whole "mission" or "community" required, the language of shared purpose can obscure who actually made the call, and why. This has been lived, not just observed academically — a mission-driven organization used community and calling language to cover what was functionally a decision by specific people to remove a dissenting voice.

## Interpretation

This is the practical, first-hand version of a named critique in the research: Gareth Morgan's "harmony trap" — biological and community metaphors imply a functional unity organizations don't actually have, and that gap can be used, deliberately or not, to avoid naming a decision as a decision. It also connects to the repository's own hard rule against euphemism (see `about_me.md`: "we're restructuring" vs. "we're cutting two roles"). "The organization resisted" or "the mission required it" is the same move as "restructuring" — grammatically true, functionally a way of not saying who decided and who was harmed.

## Practical implication

When describing organizational behaviour using living-systems language, the vocabulary should stay available for growth, learning, and adaptation. The moment the description covers a harm, name the person who decided, not the system that "responded." Living systems don't decide. People inside them do — see the repository's own Principle #4, "People Give Meaning to Systems."
