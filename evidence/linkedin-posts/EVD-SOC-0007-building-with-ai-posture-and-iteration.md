# Building with AI — Posture and Iteration — LinkedIn Posts

## Summary

Posts developing Elizabeth's personal posture toward working with AI — as a builder and as a practitioner. This cluster includes the most personal posts in the social corpus: AI as creative space restorer (July 8), the origin of Northlight OS from a webinar and a blank ClickUp workspace (July 13), and the MCP skills market as "functional is the starting point" product philosophy (July 20). These posts show the practice behind the advice.

## Source

- Channel: Elizabeth Anne (Randell) Lemoine, LinkedIn personal + company page
- Retrieved via: Buffer API
- Date range: May 2026 – July 2026
- Post count: 7 substantive posts in this cluster

## Key Claims

- Quality of thinking going in matters more than the tool; AI doesn't fix unclear thinking, it amplifies it; if you can't explain the problem to another person in plain language, you won't get a useful answer
- No employee could do five jobs at once — so why do we ask one AI agent to? The design question matters: task allocation, context provision, accountability for outputs
- AI as thinking partner to argue with, not first draft to polish; the risk isn't trusting AI too much with execution — it's trusting AI too much with problem definition; problem definition is the highest-risk phase
- AI gave back creative space: "the thing I didn't expect was that it gave me more room to think, not less — the scaffolding moved faster, and I could spend more time on the parts that actually required my judgment"; this is rarely how AI productivity is marketed but it's how it actually worked
- Northlight OS was built from scratch on ClickUp + Claude, after a webinar; "I took the webinar thinking I'd pick up one or two things. I left thinking about how I run everything"; built with the same tools Northlight uses with clients
- MCP tools as Northlight market product: "functional is the starting point, not the product — the real use is in the gap between what it can do and how it fits into the way you actually work; that gap only closes through real use"; built all three as internal tools first
- The right question before any AI integration: "what problem is this actually solving, and will this solution still make sense in six months?" — not "what can we automate?"
