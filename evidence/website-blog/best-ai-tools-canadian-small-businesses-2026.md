# Best AI Tools for Canadian Small Businesses (2026) — Northlight

## Summary

An honest, Canada-specific AI tools guide written for businesses subject to PIPEDA and Quebec Law 25. Frames the evaluation criteria as data residency and cross-border transfer risk, not feature sets. Covers general AI, meeting transcription, voice dictation, and coding tools with specific Canadian-hosted recommendations.

## Key Claims

- Most AI tools guides are written for US markets and ignore PIPEDA and Quebec Law 25. Cross-border data transfer obligations apply to Canadian businesses using US-based tools.
- Canadian-built and Canadian-hosted options now exist in most categories. They are not always as polished or cheap as US alternatives, but they exist and are getting better.
- Practical rule for US tools (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini): use for internal drafting, brainstorming, and administrative work. Do not enter identifiable client data, health information, or anything covered by PIPEDA without disclosure.
- On-device processing is the gold standard for sensitive data: audio never crosses any border. OpenWhispr (Windows, MIT-licensed, free) and VoiceInk (Mac Apple Silicon, one-time purchase under $40) are the recommended local options.
- Wispr Flow is flagged: US-based service with a documented 2025 data disclosure incident affecting user audio. Not appropriate for anything covered by PIPEDA without explicit disclosure and consent.
- SaySo is flagged: privacy policy discloses data may transfer to servers governed by PRC law — rule out for any professional use involving client information.
- Quebec Law 25 bar is higher than PIPEDA: may require a formal privacy impact assessment before sending personal data outside Quebec.
- Canadian-specific recommendations: Augure (general AI, Canadian-sovereign), Fellow (meeting notes, Ottawa, AWS Canada Central), NorthMail/NorthCloud (email/storage, Quebec City, $0.99 CAD/month).
