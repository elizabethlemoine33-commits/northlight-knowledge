# Governance Works When It Enables Responsible Action

When controls are unclear, too broad, or disconnected from practice, people route around them — creating the exact visibility problems governance was designed to prevent. Shadow systems are not defiance; they are evidence of unmet operational need.

Governance that works is proportionate, owned, and legible to the people it governs. Privacy-first and agent-authority design are extensions of this: reduce retained risk by not accumulating what you do not need and not granting autonomy beyond what you can supervise.

For AI governance specifically, the legibility question extends to trust: frameworks that answer "is this legal and safe?" but not "do people trust this enough to use it well, and trust the environment enough to say something when something doesn't seem right?" will produce the same outcome as no framework — shadow use, silent failure, and invisible risk. Governance that only appears at an incident is too late. People adopt new systems at the speed of their trust in the outcome, not the speed of the rollout plan.

This understanding should continue to accumulate supporting evidence.
