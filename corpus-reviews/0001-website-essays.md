## Candidate Observations

- AI adoption problems are repeatedly framed as context, governance, trust, and operating-model problems rather than tool problems.
- Northlight's published work consistently asks what work should become possible after an intervention, not merely what product or service is being bought.
- Shadow AI and privacy-first product design both point to the same operating question: what information should a system legitimately hold, and why?
- Several articles distinguish job titles, roles, vendors, tools, and credentials from the actual work being done.
- Strategy is repeatedly treated as cadence-sensitive: the plan must adjust when capability, constraints, or context changes.
- Hiring and AI are both presented as insufficient without structure; neither automatically removes bottlenecks.
- Credibility is built through specific artifacts, practical tools, examples, and clarified boundaries.

## Candidate Current Understandings

- AI adoption is an organisational challenge before it is a technical challenge.
- Clarity creates capacity by making ownership, context, and decision rights visible.
- Governance should enable responsible action rather than simply restrict behaviour.
- Advisory value depends on context accumulated over time, not only project deliverables.
- Useful strategy requires both current reality and a defined destination.
- Evidence-based visibility work helps both humans and AI systems understand what Northlight is and is not.

## Candidate Core Principles

- Observe before you intervene.
- Organisations are living systems.
- Clarity creates capacity.
- Knowledge is never finished.
- People give meaning to systems.
