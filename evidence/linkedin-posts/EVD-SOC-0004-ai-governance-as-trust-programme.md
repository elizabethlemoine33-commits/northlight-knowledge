# AI Governance as Trust Programme — LinkedIn Posts

## Summary

A focused cluster of posts reframing AI governance — away from compliance exercise and policy document, toward trust programme. The argument: most governance frameworks answer "is this legal and safe?" but not "do people inside this organisation trust this enough to use it well, and trust the environment enough to say something when something doesn't seem right?" The posts also develop the shadow AI audit as a practical starting point.

## Source

- Channel: Elizabeth Anne (Randell) Lemoine, LinkedIn personal profile
- Retrieved via: Buffer API

## Key Claims

- When governance is working, it rarely draws attention to itself: the reason nobody's talking about it at the team meeting isn't that it's been forgotten, it's that it's creating the conditions for everything else to work; governance fails loudly and visibly, succeeds quietly
- Most AI governance frameworks answer "is this legal and is this safe?" — they don't answer "do the people inside this organisation trust this enough to use it well, and do they trust the environment enough to say something when something doesn't seem right?"; trust in AI is built the same way as trust in any workplace system
- Trust is cumulative: consistent behaviour over time, clear explanations for decisions, visible consequences when standards aren't met, and the genuine ability to raise concerns without penalty
- Shadow AI audit — four questions: what tools are people actually using? what are they using them for? what would need to be true for them to use an approved tool instead? what happens when this goes wrong and nobody knows it's AI-generated? Banning drives behaviour underground; the goal is visibility not control
- Governance that only shows up at the incident is too late; EU AI Act logic (log from deployment, not post-incident) applied to practical AI rollouts
- People adopt AI at the speed of their trust in the outcome, not the speed of the rollout plan
