# The Wall on Day 200: When Plans Don't Go to Plan — Northlight

## Summary

Why the constraints that kill your plan only show up once you're close enough to bump into them — and the small habit that gets you there sooner.

## Key Claims

- Structural constraints (legal, regulatory, market reality) usually only reveal themselves around day 200, not day one — a sketch doesn't have a regulator; a pitch deck doesn't have an insurance underwriter
- Regular checkpoints are not strategy sessions — they are a small built-in habit: "Is this still the thing? Is the thing still possible? Has anything I assumed three months ago turned out not to be true?"
- Sunk cost makes you bad at the gut check — once a year is in, the question "is this still right?" becomes a question about whether you're the kind of person who finishes what they start, not a clean assessment of whether the plan still holds
- When the wall appears: grieve the original plan briefly and specifically before pivoting; skipping this step causes the new plan to be the old plan in a slightly different costume
- The lesson is not "be more careful" — it is to build in the looking up, make it small, make it regular, make it cost an hour not a year
