# What Is a Fractional Executive? A Guide for Canadian Businesses — Northlight

## Summary

A definition and category guide for fractional executive work in the Canadian market. Distinguishes fractional executives from consultants and contractors on the basis of ownership and accountability rather than hours or pay structure. Covers the four main types (CMO, CFO, CTO, COO) and what each one solves.

## Key Claims

- The distinction between fractional executive and consultant is not about hours or pay structure — it is about ownership. A consultant delivers advice; a fractional executive owns the function, makes decisions, builds the systems, and carries accountability for results.
- A contractor does specific tactical work; a fractional executive owns the strategy, builds the systems, makes hiring and firing decisions, and trains the team that will eventually take over.
- The right type of fractional executive matches the current bottleneck: CMO if leads aren't predictable, CFO if decisions are made without financial data, CTO if product roadmap is broken, COO if operations are breaking under growth.
- The clearest signal for which function you need: which function is currently being run by someone who is visibly out of their depth?
- The fractional market has matured: North American practitioners roughly doubled from 60,000 to 120,000 between 2022 and 2024; LinkedIn mentions grew 5,400% in the same period.
- The Canadian market particularly benefits: smaller talent pool than US cities, same operational challenges, but fractional makes senior expertise accessible without relocation or Bay Area compensation.
- A good fractional engagement has a built-in end date both parties are working toward — the goal is to make the executive unnecessary, not to create dependency.
- Not right for: pre-revenue businesses, situations requiring daily in-person firefighting, businesses that already have clear strategy and just need execution.
