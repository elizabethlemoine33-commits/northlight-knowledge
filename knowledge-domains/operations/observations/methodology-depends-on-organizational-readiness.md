# Methodology Depends on Organizational Readiness

## Observation

A sound operating methodology can perform poorly when adopted before the organisation has sufficient strategic clarity, relational trust, and implementation discipline to use it well. The methodology is not the primary variable.

## Interpretation

The conditions that make a method work — clear objectives, honest reporting, named ownership, willingness to deprioritise, visible accountability — must exist in the organisation before the method is introduced. Introducing the method does not create those conditions. OKR implementations that fail frequently do so because of implementation context, not because OKRs are a bad idea.

## Evidence

- `../../../evidence/northlight/eos-rocks-okr-retrospective.md` — direct retrospective comparison of EOS Rocks (successful in a high-trust, well-owned environment) and OKR implementation (unsuccessful in a low-trust environment with unclear strategic linkage).
