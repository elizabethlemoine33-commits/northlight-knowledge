# Working Conceptual Structure

**Do not treat this as final.**

This is the first synthesis of the interview, before Elizabeth's review and before external research.

## Central thesis

> **Operations is the backbone of an organization: the infrastructure and nervous system through which people, information, decisions, commitments, and learning move.**

Operations Built for Growth means designing that backbone not merely to support the organization that exists today, but to help the organization become what it is capable of becoming — while retaining the ability to respond intelligently when reality changes.

## Working spiral

### Awareness
Look beyond the immediate function:
- across the organization;
- toward clients and the market;
- toward the wider world.

Awareness asks: **What is happening around us?**

### Sense
Use questions, observation, data, conversations, and curiosity to understand what is actually happening.

Sense asks:
- What does this mean?
- What are we missing?
- What assumption may no longer be true?
- Where is the actual constraint?

### Build
Turn understanding into capability:
- processes;
- policies;
- systems;
- technology;
- training;
- data;
- governance;
- dashboards;
- operating rhythms.

Build asks:

> **What needs to change, and what will help the organization become what it is becoming?**

### Connect
Make sure changes and commitments work across the whole organization.

Connect asks:
- Who needs to know?
- Who owns this?
- What other system is affected?
- Does the information reach the right place?
- Does the fix create another silo?

### Learn
Turn experience into organizational memory and improved capability.

Learn asks:
- What happened?
- Why?
- What did we get right?
- What did we get wrong?
- What changes now?
- What should we do differently next time?

The cycle is a **spiral**, not a circle. Each iteration begins with more knowledge than the previous one.

## Five candidate principles

1. **See the Whole System**
2. **Build for What the Organization Is Becoming**
3. **Connect the Organization to Reality**
4. **Create Clarity, Trust, and Accountability**
5. **Turn Experience into a Spiral of Learning**

These titles are provisional.

## Market-Aware COO — working definition

> **A Market-Aware COO is a generalist integrator who looks beyond the boundaries of their own function — across the organization, toward clients and the market, and outward toward a changing world — then turns what they learn into better decisions, adaptive systems, and greater organizational capability.**

Important: this definition is a working formulation, not yet ratified.

## Conditions around the spiral

These are not necessarily steps:
- curiosity;
- humility;
- trust;
- accountability;
- psychological safety;
- appropriate risk;
- clarity;
- willingness to innovate.

## Tensions to preserve

The framework should avoid false binaries:
- strong vs. flexible → adaptive;
- control vs. freedom → appropriate constraints;
- trust vs. verification → trust and verify;
- expertise vs. generalism → integrated generalist with subject-matter experts;
- present vs. future → build for becoming;
- efficiency vs. growth → operational effectiveness in service of the organization's trajectory.

## The 10x problem

"Scalable" should not become shorthand for "10x."

A growth-ready system needs to consider:
- what changes at greater scale;
- what breaks;
- where information flow fails;
- how decision rights change;
- how client experience changes;
- how cash flow changes;
- how staffing/capacity changes;
- how risk changes;
- how the organization itself needs to become different.

The goal is not to predict the future perfectly.

The goal is **adaptive capacity**.
