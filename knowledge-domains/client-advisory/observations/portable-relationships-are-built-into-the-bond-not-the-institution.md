# Portable relationships are built into the bond, not borrowed from the institution

## Observation

Not all contextual relationships are built the same way. Relationships that don't survive the removal of their context — the end of a job, a change in proximity, the close of an engagement — were held together primarily by the institutional context: the shared employer, the daily proximity, the common project. Remove the institution and there is nothing load-bearing underneath.

Relationships that survive context removal are built differently: the context lives in the bond itself rather than in the institution. Shared interests, genuine mutual curiosity about each other's separate lives, families, the small ongoing gestures that don't require a shared workplace to make sense — these create a context that travels. Neither party has to be good at frequent contact; the relationship picks up because the foundation doesn't depend on regularity or proximity to stay intact.

The distinction is not about effort — it is about where the context was built.

## Interpretation

For advisory practice, this is a design question. A client relationship built only on shared work will not survive the engagement ending — the work was the entire context. A relationship built on the person — their experiences, their family, their interests outside the work, the kinds of exchanges that land naturally even when there is no active project — has context that outlasts the engagement.

The practical implication is an approach: learn about the person, not just the problem. Find the "appropriate variation of the same topics" — what you'd learn about any person you intend to know for a long time. A message about something completely off-topic sent months after an engagement closes should land naturally, not feel like an awkward reach. If it would feel like an awkward reach, the relationship was built on the institution.

This is structurally parallel to how good operational systems are designed: the logic is built into the structure, not left in the designer's head. Relationships designed to last are built into the bond, not left in the institution.

## Evidence Needed

- Retrospective comparison of which professional relationships have survived context removal and what distinguished them from those that didn't.
- Evidence on whether deliberate relationship-building practices (learning about families, maintaining non-work contact) correlate with post-engagement relationship continuity.
- Counterexamples: relationships built primarily on shared work that survived because the shared work was deep enough to constitute a genuine bond.
