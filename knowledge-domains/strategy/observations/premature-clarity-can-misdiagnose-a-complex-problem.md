# Premature clarity can misdiagnose a complex problem as a complicated one

## Observation

The organisational clarity sequence — clarify outcomes, ownership, decisions, information flow, measures — assumes the problem in front of the client is knowable through analysis and has a right answer once analyzed. That assumption holds for complicated problems. Applied to a genuinely complex one (new market, ambiguous mandate, a team still discovering what the actual problem is), clarifying ownership and measures too early can produce false confidence rather than real capacity, and can strip out the variety — redundant roles, informal workarounds, unresolved ambiguity — the organisation may need to handle what's coming.

## Interpretation

This is the practical version of two research findings: Cynefin's warning that organizations in the "Clear" domain become complacent and brittle because early clarity suppresses the sensing needed to notice a shifting environment, and Ashby's law of requisite variety — a system's capacity to respond to its environment is bounded by its own internal variety, so simplifying below what the environment actually demands doesn't create capacity, it removes the repertoire needed to handle what shows up. Northlight's clarity framework already hedges against the crude version of this ("unnecessary complexity," not all complexity) — the gap is that the framework doesn't yet have an explicit step that checks whether the problem is complicated or complex before applying the clarity sequence.

## Practical implication

Before running the organisational clarity framework, ask whether cause and effect in this situation are knowable in advance (complicated — clarity work is the right move) or only knowable in retrospect (complex — probe with small, safe-to-fail experiments first, and let clarity emerge from what's learned, per Weick's sensemaking: understanding often follows action rather than preceding it). Northlight's existing "observe before you intervene" posture already does this sensing; it just isn't yet a named checkpoint inside the clarity framework itself.
