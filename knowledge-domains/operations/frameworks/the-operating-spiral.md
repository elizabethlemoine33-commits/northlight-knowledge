# The Operating Spiral

## Core Idea

Operational improvement is not a circle that returns an organisation to the same starting point.

It is a spiral.

Each cycle builds on what the team has already learned. The next round begins with a different understanding of the situation because experience, evidence, mistakes, successes, and relationships have accumulated.

The organisation does not return to the ground floor. It climbs to the next level.

## The Spiral

**Understand → Clarify → Prioritize → Define → Create → Execute → Learn → Adjust → Understand again**

The second "Understand" is not the same understanding as the first. It incorporates everything learned on the previous level.

### Understand

What is actually happening? What is working? What is not? What do people know? What remains unclear?

Understanding includes context, history, evidence, constraints, and the lived experience of the people doing the work.

### Clarify

What are we trying to accomplish? Why does it matter? What does success or "good enough" look like?

### Prioritize

What matters now?

This is also where the organisation deliberately decides what does not matter now. Important ideas can be retained without becoming active commitments.

### Define

What needs to happen? Who owns it? Who is accountable? What evidence will show progress? What dependencies or boundaries matter?

### Create

Build the process, system, capability, experiment, artifact, or other mechanism needed to advance the objective.

### Execute

Do the work. Make progress visible. Surface obstacles early.

### Learn

What happened? What surprised us? What worked? What did not? What did the attempt teach us?

Learning from an unsuccessful attempt is itself a form of success when the learning improves the next decision.

### Adjust

Keep it. Change it. Stop it. Try again. Reprioritize. Reframe the objective. Or move it to the parking lot.

Then begin the next level of the spiral with the benefit of everything learned before.

## Trust Is the Handrail

Trust sits alongside the spiral rather than inside it.

It allows people to:

- tell the truth about problems;
- admit uncertainty;
- ask for help;
- challenge priorities;
- take ownership;
- experiment safely;
- recover when something does not work;
- share learning without fear of punishment.

Without trust, the spiral becomes performative. People hide problems, protect metrics, avoid difficult questions, and optimise for appearing successful.

## Management Implication

A manager's job is not to prevent every failure. It is to create conditions in which people can succeed: clear expectations, appropriate ownership, useful resources, accessible support, honest feedback, and room to learn.

## Important Boundary

The Operating Spiral is an execution and learning model. It does not create organisational vision.

Leadership must establish the destination. Operations can help translate that destination into priorities, ownership, systems, sequencing, and execution.

Execution can create momentum. It cannot create direction.
