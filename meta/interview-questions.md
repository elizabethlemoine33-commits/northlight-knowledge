# Interview Questions — Pulling Unresolved Threads

These questions exist because the knowledge base distills thinking to an essence that may not represent the full complexity of the views behind it. They are intended to be asked in interview, conversation, or recorded reflection — not answered in writing.

---

## On resistance and grief

**"You've said resistance usually points to missing information. Have you been in a room where you gave people everything — the structure, the clarity, the timeline, the reason — and they still resisted? What was actually happening there, and did you do anything different?"**

*Why this matters:* The library treats resistance as almost always cognitive — an information or structure problem. But loss aversion and grief are real. This question tests whether the structural frame holds in all cases or only most.

---

## On clarity and trust sequencing

**"You've said clarity is kindness, and also that clarity can land as threat without enough trust. What do you actually do when you can't build the trust first — when the engagement is short, or the stakes are high, or the relationship is new? Do you soften the clarity, or do you proceed and accept the discomfort?"**

*Why this matters:* The library has the tension but not the resolution. The practical answer — what do you actually do — is missing.

---

## On meaning vs. belonging

**"You believe work can be meaningful. What makes it meaningful for you — is it the quality of the outcome, the difficulty of the problem, the people who benefit from it, or something else? And is that the same thing you'd point clients to, or is meaning at work different for a founder than for the people they lead?"**

*Why this matters:* The library is light on psychology and organizational behaviour. Meaning is agreed to be real; belonging and authentic workplace relationships are more contested. This question pulls on where the line is.

---

## On contextual relationships

**"The relationships you expected to last after leaving a context — the ones that have basically fallen apart despite effort — what do you think they were made of? And does that change how you think about what you're building with clients now?"**

*Why this matters:* The observation that relationships are contextual — real while in context, hard to sustain after — is more precise than either "belonging matters" or "relationships don't matter." The library has no frame for this yet.

**"The organizational behaviour literature says genuine belonging and authentic workplace relationships are necessary for performance. Your experience seems to be that work relationships are real but contextual — they don't need to be portable to have mattered. What do you think the research is getting wrong, or what is it measuring that you're not experiencing?"**

*Why this matters:* This view diverges from mainstream org psych and deserves a 'why' — not just the position but the reasoning. If the counter-argument is "belonging research measures something different from what I'm describing," that's a defensible and interesting claim. If it's "I've just had different experiences," that's honest but a different kind of evidence. The distinction matters for how confidently the library can hold this.

**"If you believe work relationships are primarily contextual, what does that mean for clients who are lonely at the top — who present to you as needing a thinking partner as much as an advisor? Are you selling them something that's contextual too, and do you think about what happens to that when the engagement ends?"**

*Why this matters:* Pulls on the advisory model directly. If client relationships are also contextual, the exit question becomes part of the engagement design, not an afterthought.

---

## On the advisory accumulation tension

**"The founder who's the only one in the room who can make the call is a structural problem you help fix. But your advisory value is partly that you've built irreplaceable context about that client's business. How is that different?"**

*Why this matters:* The operations domain argues systems shouldn't depend on any one person. The advisory model depends on accumulated context. The library doesn't resolve this.

---

## On AI context vs. capability

**"You've argued that AI underperformance is almost always a context problem. Have you hit cases where the context was good and the tool still didn't do what you needed — where the constraint was the model, not the organisation? What did that look like?"**

*Why this matters:* The library puts almost all causation on the organisational side. This question tests whether there are genuine tool-side limits that the current frame misses.

---

## On measurement

**"You ask 'what will be different in six months?' at the start of every engagement. At the end of six months, how do you actually check? And have you ever gotten to the end and found the answer was: nothing is different, and it wasn't your fault?"**

*Why this matters:* The library is strong on diagnosis and structural design and nearly silent on outcome measurement. This question surfaces what measurement actually looks like in practice — and what you do when the answer is ambiguous.

---

## On Maslow and work

**"You believe in Maslow's Hierarchy. Do you think work is the right context to solve for the higher needs — belonging, esteem, self-actualization — or do you think work is better understood as the context that handles the lower tiers, freeing people to find the upper ones elsewhere?"**

*Why this matters:* The library has almost no explicit position on what work is for, beyond operational and structural claims. This question reaches into the values layer.

*Note: These questions were generated by scanning the knowledge base for claims stated with high confidence that rest on contested or unresolved foundations. The goal is not to undermine the library's positions but to stress-test them and capture the more nuanced views that a distilled knowledge base tends to lose.*
