# AI governance is a trust programme, not a compliance exercise

## Observation

Most AI governance frameworks answer: is this legal? Is this safe? They do not answer: do the people inside this organisation trust AI enough to use it well? Do they trust the environment enough to say something when something doesn't seem right?

Trust in AI is built the same way as trust in any workplace system — consistent behaviour over time, clear explanations for decisions, visible consequences when standards aren't met, and the genuine ability to raise concerns without penalty. Governance that only appears at an incident is too late.

People adopt AI at the speed of their trust in the outcome, not the speed of the rollout plan.

## Interpretation

This reframes the governance conversation from risk management to enablement. A governance framework that people cannot use or do not trust produces the same outcome as no framework: shadow use, silent failure, and invisible risk.

The practical implication is that governance design must include: how will people know what good use looks like? Who can they ask when something feels off? What happens when they raise a concern? These questions are not legal questions — they are operational and cultural ones.

## Evidence Needed

- Examples where governance adoption succeeded because of trust conditions, not just policy quality.
- Examples where technically compliant governance frameworks failed in practice due to trust gaps.
- Evidence on the relationship between psychological safety and AI governance compliance.
