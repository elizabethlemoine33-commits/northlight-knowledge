# Clarity as Design Principle — LinkedIn Posts

## Summary

A cluster of LinkedIn posts articulating "clarity is kindness" as an operating philosophy — not a personality trait or communication style. Clarity is presented as a design choice with structural consequences: it removes ambiguity, creates accountability, and generates the conditions for people to take ownership. The posts also surface an important tension: clarity can feel threatening to people who have relied on ambiguity as protection.

## Source

- Channel: Elizabeth Anne (Randell) Lemoine, LinkedIn personal profile
- Retrieved via: Buffer API
- Date range: December 2025 – June 2026
- Post count: ~10 substantive posts in this cluster

## Key Claims

- "Clarity is kindness" is a design principle, not a personality trait — operations done well create space for meaningful work; they are felt even when they go unnoticed
- Organizational problems are often about language: when teams don't share definitions, assumptions, or decision criteria, even good intentions collide; shared language creates the conditions for creativity
- Good operations are a form of care — respect for people's time, attention, and effort expressed through thoughtful structure
- Clarity increases accountability, and some people experience that increase as exposure rather than support; building enough trust that the standard feels reachable is a distinct piece of work from establishing the standard itself
- Too much structure doesn't limit people — it does the opposite; structure removes guesswork and creates the conditions for people to take ownership and produce better work
- More complexity accumulates without decisions: "a weak system will collapse under a sophisticated tool — now the dysfunction has a dashboard"
- The most useful thing a leadership team can do is agree on what they are NOT doing; "we'll get to that in Q3" is usually code for "we haven't made the call yet"
- Three signs a strategy document is hiding the problem: every priority is labeled "high"; the document requires a slide deck to make sense; nobody references it after the offsite
- Clarity without context can feel like judgment: the goal is not to soften the clarity but to build enough trust that the standard feels like something people can reach for, not something designed to expose them
