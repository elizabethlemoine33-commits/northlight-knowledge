# Fractional Advisory and Founder Structural Patterns — LinkedIn Posts

## Summary

A cluster of posts developing the commercial and structural case for fractional advisory in the Canadian market — with significant new content not covered in the blog corpus. Key additions: the "three types of founders who work with Northlight" as a qualifying frame, the Atlantic Canada COO math articulated in plain numbers, and the distinction between strategy consultants and implementation partners as a client education tool.

## Key Claims

- First question in every engagement: "If this goes well, what will be different in six months?" — not deliverables, not metrics, but what is materially different for the people doing the work and how decisions get made; this becomes the reference point for everything else
- "The only person in the room who can make the call" — not because no one else is capable but because no one else has the context; all of that context lives in one head, and somehow that became the job; this is the structural problem fractional is designed to solve
- Three types of founders who work with Northlight: (1) have built something real and can feel the ops constraints beginning to affect the business; (2) are doing well but are starting to feel the cognitive load of holding it all themselves; (3) know they need fractional support and are figuring out what that looks like in practice
- Atlantic Canada COO math: a full-time COO in Atlantic Canada runs $150K–$200K/year all-in; a fractional engagement runs $3,000–$6,000/month; the fractional is designed for intermittent ops work — the kind that doesn't justify a full-time hire but genuinely can't be skipped; the math is sustainable at $5M and below because of how the work is actually structured
- "Most Canadian businesses that have 'tried AI' have actually hired a strategy consultant, received a deck full of recommendations, and been left to implement those recommendations themselves" — strategy consultants and implementation partners are not the same category; the test: can they show you a project write-up with the problem, the solution, and measurable results?
- Hiring someone competent is supposed to solve the problem; it doesn't — not automatically; the bottleneck doesn't move, it multiplies; the structural work that most founders skip because no one told them it was part of the job
