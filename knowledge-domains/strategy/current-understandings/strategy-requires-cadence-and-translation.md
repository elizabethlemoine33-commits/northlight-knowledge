# Strategy Requires Cadence and Translation

A strategy that is not reviewed regularly and does not produce next actions is aspirational decoration. The gap between stated priorities and daily operating decisions is where most strategic drift occurs.

Translation is the critical step: converting direction into specific decisions, visible checkpoints, and named owners. Without it, the strategy exists only in presentations and does not reach the work.

A strategy document that has not forced a prioritisation trade-off is not a strategy — it is a list of aspirations. Three signals that a document is hiding the problem: every priority is labeled "high"; the document requires a slide deck to be understood; nobody references it after the offsite. The most useful thing a leadership team can do is agree explicitly on what they are not doing — the absence of a stated no means every competing priority remains viable.

This understanding should continue to accumulate supporting evidence.
