# Validation Questions

This document separates Elizabeth's lived practitioner observations from claims that may eventually be presented as broader theory.

## 1. Operations and organizational climate

Observation:
Elizabeth has experienced the operating leader's emotional state as influencing the team's emotional state.

Research questions:
- What evidence exists around leader affect and emotional contagion?
- How does leader behaviour affect organizational climate?
- What is known about psychological safety and operational leadership?

## 2. Operations and trust

Observation:
Consistent operational accountability, including the willingness to say no when appropriate, can increase trust.

Research questions:
- What does organizational research say about procedural trust?
- Does predictable enforcement of legitimate boundaries increase trust?
- How does transparent risk management affect employee trust?

## 3. Learning from failure and trust

Observation:
Owning a failure, apologizing, explaining the cause, changing the system, and behaving differently can preserve or strengthen trust.

Research questions:
- What evidence exists around service recovery and trust?
- What is known about organizational learning after operational failures?
- Under what conditions does transparency after failure improve reputation?

## 4. Cross-functional integration

Observation:
As SMBs grow, informal communication stops being sufficient and intentional cross-functional information flow becomes more important.

Research questions:
- What organizational research supports this transition?
- Are there known thresholds where coordination mechanisms need to change?
- What does the literature say about cross-functional teams and information flow?

## 5. Market orientation and operations

Observation:
Operations should incorporate market/client intelligence rather than treating marketing as a separate department.

Research questions:
- What does market-orientation research say?
- How do customer insights influence operational performance?
- Is there evidence that integrating customer intelligence into operations improves growth or retention?

## 6. Adaptive systems

Observation:
The desired operational system is not simply strong or scalable; it is adaptive.

Research questions:
- What is the relationship between organizational resilience and adaptive capacity?
- How do adaptive organizations balance standardization with flexibility?
- What systems-design literature supports this distinction?

## 7. Generalist COO / integrator role

Observation:
A COO's value can come from integration, questioning, facilitation, and cross-functional understanding rather than deep subject-matter expertise.

Research questions:
- How is the COO role described in established literature?
- What is known about integrator roles?
- How do generalist leaders create value in complex organizations?

## 8. Growth and operational architecture

Observation:
Building systems only for current scale can constrain future growth.

Research questions:
- What evidence exists around process debt and organizational scaling?
- How do information architectures need to evolve with company size?
- What is known about organizational stage transitions?

## 9. Spiral / organizational learning

Observation:
Learning should be represented as an upward spiral rather than a flat loop because each iteration changes the starting condition.

Research questions:
- What organizational learning models use iterative or spiral structures?
- How do continuous improvement systems encode prior learning?
- Is there useful literature from systems thinking, double-loop learning, or adaptive cycles?

## 10. Claims that should remain explicitly experiential until researched

Do not currently state these as universal facts:
- "As the COO goes, the organization goes."
- "Healthy operations causes psychological safety."
- "A single failure handled well can increase trust."
- "Customer concentration is a marketing failure."
- "Operations is the backbone of every organization."
- "The COO is uniquely positioned to see cross-functional seams."

These can remain as Elizabeth's practitioner propositions until supported, refined, or challenged by research.
