# Elizabeth Lemoine — CliftonStrengths 34 Profile & Operating-Style Evidence

## Summary

Elizabeth's CliftonStrengths 34 results (Learner, Analytical, Deliberative, Relator, Intellection, Input, Achiever, Restorative, Individualization, Focus — top 10, in rank order) describe a Strategic Thinking-led, Executing-heavy operator with almost no natural Influencing-domain presence (Woo, Communication, Positivity, Activator all sit in the bottom eight of 34). Reviewed against her actual career arc and in her own words during a 2026-07-31 conversation, the profile doesn't just describe a personality type — it explains specific, previously unarticulated mechanics of how she operates: why "trusted #2 to a founder" is a better positioning than "visionary out front," why she reads as adaptable in a live-events crisis without the personality traits usually associated with adaptability, and why her HR-adjacent work is a policy/systems fit rather than an HR Business Partner fit.

**Note on evidence quality:** this is a single self-report instrument taken 2018-02-20, now 8 years old. CliftonStrengths themes are considered stable over adult life by Gallup's own research, and Elizabeth's 2026 commentary in the source conversation confirms the mechanics still match current behaviour — but this should be treated as corroborated self-report, not behavioural/360 data. Confidence is set to medium on that basis, not because the pattern seems wrong.

## The Profile

**Domain lead:** Strategic Thinking (Learner, Analytical, Intellection, Input all in top 6; Strategic itself sits at #12).

**Top 10, in order:** Learner, Analytical, Deliberative, Relator, Intellection, Input, Achiever, Restorative, Individualization, Focus.

**Bottom 8 (least natural):** Woo (34), Positivity (33), Communication (32), Adaptability (31), Maximizer (30), Includer (29), Empathy (28), Connectedness (27), Activator (26).

No Influencing-domain theme appears in the top 10. Command, Self-Assurance, and Competition sit in the low-to-mid teens — enough for quiet backbone, not enough to describe someone who leads through visible presence or persuasion.

## Operating-Style Mechanics This Explains

### 1. "Trusted #2," not "visionary out front"

Deliberative + Analytical + Achiever + Restorative + Focus is the classic operations-builder combination: cautious and evidence-seeking before deciding, then relentless once committed. Relator + Individualization explain the relationship side of it — not broad organisational charisma, but one deep, earned relationship with the founder/ED who needs someone they can hand real infrastructure to. Combined with the near-total absence of Influencing themes, this is a profile built to be the operator who doesn't compete with the founder for the spotlight, not a profile built to be the public-facing visionary. This directly supports the existing "founder's trusted #2" positioning in Elizabeth's professional summary (see `operational-experience.md`) — the positioning matches durable operating-style wiring, not just career strategy.

### 2. Event "adaptability" is pre-solved contingency, not improvisation

Elizabeth's own framing (2026-07-31): live-event flexibility isn't Adaptability the theme (going with the flow, discovering the future one day at a time) — Adaptability is her 31st-ranked theme, near the bottom. What looks like composure under live-event pressure is Deliberative doing its normal work upstream: build the plan, the backup plan, and the backup to the backup, so that switching branches in the moment is executing a pre-written contingency, not inventing one. This reframes the events/operations track: her strength there is the planning-and-risk backbone (Deliberative/Restorative/Achiever), consciously built to let someone with genuine Woo/Positivity/Communication take the front of the room — which lines up with the "operational backbone, creating space for those whose talents should be showcased" framing she used directly.

### 3. Individualization, not Empathy or Woo, is the relationship mechanism

Elizabeth describes deliberately building 1:1 relationships by holding specific, retrievable details about people and deploying them later so they feel seen — remembered specifics, not emotional attunement or broad-room charisma. This maps directly to Individualization (#9: intrigued by each person's unique qualities, sees how different people can work together) rather than Empathy (#28, near bottom) or Woo (#34, last). It's a more precise and more differentiable story than "good with people": it's an observation-and-recall mechanism, evidenced in the "legendary" thank-you letters described in `VOICE_PROFILE_Elizabeth_Lemoine.md` (naming individuals and specific contributions from memory) and consistent with the Relator pattern of a small, deliberately deep circle rather than a wide network.

### 4. HR fit is policy/systems, not HRBP

Relator and Individualization are real assets for case-by-case judgment and policy design. But classic HR Business Partner work — being the org-wide empathetic front door, driving culture/engagement campaigns, reading a room's emotional temperature — leans on Empathy, Includer, and Positivity, all in Elizabeth's bottom eight. This corroborates, from an independent instrument, the scoping already established in the career-counsellor skill: lead with HR-adjacent systems, onboarding, and policy work, not dedicated HRBP positioning.

### 5. Competition (#15) is the real Influencing signature — and it's a calibration tool, not a display

Competition is Elizabeth's highest-ranked Influencing-domain theme — ahead of Self-Assurance
(#16), and well ahead of Command (#20), Significance (#25), Activator (#26), Communication
(#32), and Woo (#34). That ordering matters: this isn't about wanting to be seen as important
(Significance), taking visible control of a room (Command), or persuading through charisma
(Woo, Communication). Elizabeth's own account (2026-07-31): she plays a recurring LinkedIn game
and specifically tracks her performance against one consistent competitor — a PhD holder who
often beats her by fractions of a second — checking not just who won but the exact margin, every
time. The mechanism isn't "I need to win" so much as "I need a benchmark to know whether I
actually did well." Competition functions here as a personal calibration tool, not a public
display — worth keeping in exactly that specificity rather than generalizing it into "competitive
by nature."

### 6. The natural pace-vs-speed tension

Learner/Analytical/Deliberative/Intellection dominate the top 10, meaning Elizabeth is wired to want more information before deciding than a fast-moving context sometimes allows. Achiever and Focus close that gap once she's committed — but the tension is real and worth naming proactively (e.g., in interviews, or in content about decision-making under ambiguity) rather than treated as a gap to hide.
